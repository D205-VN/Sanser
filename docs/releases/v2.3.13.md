# Sanser 2.3.13

This update targets stutter when a phone views a Mac over jittery Wi-Fi. Update
the hosting Mac; the phone app does not need to change.

- A Mac host serving a phone now drops to 2/3 size per side (1920x1080 becomes
  1280x720) once the video budget stays below 4 Mbps for 1.5 seconds. It
  returns to full size after the budget holds 5.4 Mbps or more for 6 seconds,
  and stays at least 8 seconds at each size so a jittery path does not flap.
  Each switch starts with a keyframe at the new size; iPhone and Android
  viewers already resize their decoder from each frame.
- The H.264 session is re-created at the new size and ScreenCaptureKit is
  reconfigured in place, without restarting the session. Captures still queued
  at the old size are discarded.

Why: a recorded iPhone-to-MacBook Air session on 2.3.12 showed the host
admitting only about 16 captures per second (495 encoded in 31 s), while
encoding took about 10 ms per frame. The budget had fallen from 5.3 to 3.0 Mbps
after Wi-Fi RTT spikes of 50–57 ms, and a 1080p picture of fast motion was too
large for the paced sender at that rate. The phone showed 4–24 fps with 7–19
repair requests per second.

Trade-off: when the network is weak, text and fine detail on the phone are
softer until the budget recovers.

Scope: Mac hosts with a single phone viewer only. Windows hosts, Mac or Windows
viewers and multi-computer sharing are unchanged. Wi-Fi loss and RTT spikes
are not removed by this release.

Checks: the new resolution-policy test, a VideoToolbox test that resizes the
encoder mid-stream and decodes keyframes at both sizes, and the full desktop
(47) and mobile native (6) suites passed. No physical phone session has
validated this change yet.

Mobile version: 2.3.13, build 20321. The mobile app has no functional change in
this release.
